How Much Does a Kitchen Remodel Cost? (2025 Pricing Guide)

June 2025 10 min read By Moore Quality Builders

A kitchen remodel is one of the highest-return investments you can make in your home. But before you start picking countertops and cabinet hardware, the most common question is: what's it actually going to cost?

The honest answer depends on your kitchen's size, your material choices, and the scope of work involved. This guide breaks down real 2025 costs across every major category so you can plan your budget with confidence—no guesswork, no surprises.

Kitchen Remodel Cost Tiers

Kitchen remodels generally fall into three tiers. Here's a broad overview before we go line by line:

Budget Remodel — $10,000 to $25,000

Cosmetic updates: painting cabinets, new hardware, laminate countertops, updated lighting, and basic flooring. Layout stays the same. No moving of plumbing or electrical.

Best for: Homeowners who want a fresh look without a full gut renovation.

Mid-Range Remodel — $25,000 to $60,000

New semi-custom cabinets, quartz or granite countertops, updated appliances, tile backsplash, new flooring, and recessed lighting. May include minor layout changes.

Best for: Most homeowners looking for a significant upgrade with quality materials.

High-End Remodel — $60,000 to $120,000+

Full gut renovation with custom cabinetry, premium stone countertops, professional-grade appliances, custom tile work, structural changes, and full electrical and plumbing updates.

Best for: Luxury upgrades, larger kitchens, or homes where the kitchen is a major selling feature.

Cost Breakdown by Category

Here's where your money actually goes in a typical mid-range kitchen remodel:

Estimated Cost by Component:

  • Cabinets (30–40% of budget): $5,000–$30,000+ — Stock cabinets are the most affordable; custom cabinetry can exceed $50,000
  • Labor (20–35%): $3,000–$20,000 — Varies by scope; includes demo, installation, carpentry, and finish work
  • Countertops (10–15%): $2,000–$10,000 — Laminate starts around $30/sq ft; quartz runs $70–$120/sq ft; natural stone higher
  • Appliances (10–20%): $3,000–$15,000 — Entry-level suites to professional-grade packages
  • Flooring (5–10%): $1,500–$6,000 — Tile, LVP, or hardwood depending on preference
  • Plumbing (4–7%): $1,000–$5,000 — New sink, faucet, dishwasher hookup; more if moving lines
  • Electrical (4–7%): $1,000–$5,000 — Recessed lighting, under-cabinet lighting, updated panel if needed
  • Backsplash (2–4%): $500–$3,000 — Subway tile on the low end; handmade or custom tile on the high end
  • Design & Permits (3–6%): $1,500–$4,000 — Permit fees vary by city; design services are separate

These percentages shift depending on your priorities. Clients who splurge on custom cabinetry often save on appliances and vice versa.

What Affects the Final Price

Two kitchens the same square footage can have wildly different price tags. Here's why:

Layout Changes

Moving the sink, dishwasher, or refrigerator means relocating plumbing and electrical—this adds $2,000–$8,000 or more depending on complexity. Keeping your layout saves significant money.

Kitchen Size

More square footage means more cabinets, more countertop, more flooring, more labor. A 150 sq ft kitchen will cost considerably more than a 100 sq ft kitchen with the same materials.

Material Quality

The gap between budget and premium materials is enormous. Stock IKEA cabinets vs. custom maple cabinetry can be a $20,000+ difference on their own.

Structural or Code Work

Older homes may require electrical panel upgrades, new venting for a range hood, or water line updates to bring everything to current code. This work is non-negotiable but adds to the budget.

💡 Pro Tip: The best way to control costs is to finalize your design and material selections before construction begins. Change orders mid-project are one of the biggest budget busters.

Permits & Hidden Costs

Many homeowners are surprised by costs that don't show up in a basic estimate. Here's what to watch for:

When You Need a Permit:

  • Moving or adding electrical circuits
  • Relocating plumbing lines
  • Structural changes (removing a wall, adding a window)
  • New range hood venting through exterior walls

Cosmetic work—new countertops, cabinet painting, flooring, appliance swaps—typically does not require a permit.

⚠️ Heads Up: Skipping permits on work that requires them can cause major problems when you sell your home. Buyers' inspectors and lenders will flag unpermitted work, and you may be required to bring it up to code at your own expense.

Other Costs to Budget For:

  • Demolition & disposal: $500–$2,500 to remove and haul away old cabinets, counters, and flooring
  • Temporary kitchen setup: Consider a hot plate and small fridge during construction (2–6 weeks)
  • Contingency buffer: Always add 10–15% to your budget for surprises discovered during demo (water damage, outdated wiring, etc.)

Typical Kitchen Remodel Timeline

How long you'll be without a functioning kitchen depends on scope:

Phase 1: Design & Planning (2–6 weeks)

  • Contractor consultations and estimates
  • Finalizing layout and material selections
  • Ordering cabinets (lead times can be 4–10 weeks for custom)

Phase 2: Permits (1–3 weeks)

  • Permit application submission
  • Approval (varies by city)

Phase 3: Construction (3–8 weeks)

  • Demolition (1–3 days)
  • Rough plumbing and electrical (3–5 days)
  • Cabinet installation (2–4 days)
  • Countertop templating and fabrication (1–2 weeks lead time)
  • Appliance installation, backsplash, flooring, and punch list (1–2 weeks)

Total from first call to finished kitchen: 8–20 weeks depending on scope and material availability.

Return on Investment

Kitchen remodels consistently rank among the top home improvements for resale value. According to national remodeling data:

Average ROI by Remodel Tier:

  • Minor kitchen remodel (budget): 70–80% return at resale
  • Mid-range kitchen remodel: 55–70% return at resale
  • Major upscale remodel: 40–55% return at resale

In competitive markets like Southern California, an updated kitchen significantly reduces time on market and can tip a buyer's decision between otherwise comparable homes. For homeowners who plan to stay long-term, the daily quality-of-life improvement is often worth the investment regardless of exact ROI figures.

Why a Licensed Contractor Matters

A kitchen remodel involves plumbing, electrical, structural work, and city permits. Here's what's at stake when you cut corners on who does the work:

  • Code compliance: Licensed contractors know exactly what requires a permit and how to pass inspections
  • Insurance protection: If something goes wrong on your property, you're covered
  • Quality workmanship: Properly installed cabinets, level countertops, and clean tile work make a real difference
  • Accountability: Licensed contractors are bonded, insured, and answer to the CSLB—your recourse if issues arise
  • Resale protection: Permitted, licensed work adds verifiable value and avoids complications during escrow
⚠️ Watch Out For: Unlicensed contractors offering suspiciously low bids. They often cut corners on code compliance, use substandard materials, or disappear mid-project. Verify any contractor's license at CSLB.ca.gov before signing anything.
