How Much Does a Bathroom Remodel Cost? (2025 Pricing Guide)

June 2025 9 min read By Moore Quality Builders

Bathroom remodels consistently rank among the most searched home improvement topics—and for good reason. Whether it's a dated guest bath or a master suite overdue for an upgrade, a well-done bathroom renovation pays dividends in comfort, function, and resale value.

But costs vary dramatically depending on bathroom size, fixture choices, tile selections, and scope of work. This guide breaks down real 2025 pricing across every major category so you can budget accurately before talking to a single contractor.

Bathroom Remodel Cost Tiers

Like kitchen remodels, bathroom projects fall into three general tiers based on scope and material quality:

Budget Remodel — $5,000 to $15,000

Cosmetic refresh: new vanity, toilet, faucets, light fixture, mirror, and paint. Existing tile and layout stay in place. No plumbing relocation.

Best for: Guest bathrooms, rental properties, or homeowners who need functional updates without a full gut renovation.

Mid-Range Remodel — $15,000 to $35,000

Full gut renovation: new tile throughout, updated shower or tub, new vanity with stone countertop, quality fixtures, recessed lighting, and exhaust fan. Layout generally stays the same.

Best for: Main bathrooms and master baths where quality and longevity matter.

High-End Remodel — $35,000 to $80,000+

Luxury renovation: custom tile work, walk-in shower with multiple fixtures, freestanding soaking tub, heated floors, custom cabinetry, premium fixtures, and possible layout changes or square footage expansion.

Best for: Master bathroom upgrades, luxury homes, or when the bathroom is a major selling feature.

Cost Breakdown by Category

Here's where the money goes in a typical mid-range bathroom remodel:

Estimated Cost by Component:

  • Labor (40–50% of budget): $5,000–$18,000 — Tile work, plumbing rough-in, carpentry, and finish work are highly skilled and account for the largest share
  • Tile (15–25%): $1,500–$8,000 — Floor tile, shower walls, and accent tile; cost varies enormously by material and complexity of the layout
  • Shower or Tub (10–20%): $1,500–$12,000+ — Prefab shower kits start low; custom tile showers with frameless glass doors push the high end
  • Vanity & Countertop (10–15%): $800–$6,000 — Stock vanities are affordable; custom cabinetry with stone tops adds up quickly
  • Toilet (2–5%): $300–$1,500 — Standard to comfort height to high-efficiency models
  • Fixtures & Hardware (3–6%): $500–$3,000 — Faucets, towel bars, shower head, and accessories
  • Lighting & Electrical (3–6%): $500–$2,500 — Vanity lighting, recessed fixtures, exhaust fan with timer
  • Permits & Miscellaneous (3–5%): $500–$2,000 — Demo disposal, permit fees, waterproofing membrane

Tile labor in particular is one of the most underestimated costs. Intricate patterns, large-format tile, and niche or accent work all add time and therefore cost.

Costs by Bathroom Type

Bathroom size and configuration have a major impact on total cost:

Half Bath (Powder Room) — $3,000 to $10,000

No shower or tub, just a toilet and sink. Smaller footprint means less tile and less labor. A great opportunity to make a high-design statement in a smaller budget.

Full Guest Bath — $8,000 to $25,000

Standard tub/shower combo, toilet, single vanity. Mid-size project with moderate tile work. Most common full bathroom renovation.

Master Bathroom — $20,000 to $60,000+

Larger footprint, often includes walk-in shower, soaking tub, double vanity, and more tile square footage. The most complex and costly bathroom project type.

Shower-Only Conversion — $5,000 to $18,000

Removing a tub and converting to a walk-in shower only. Popular with homeowners who never use the tub. Cost depends on tile selection and glass enclosure style.

What Drives the Final Price

Tile Selection

Tile is one of the biggest variables in bathroom pricing. Basic ceramic floor tile runs $2–$5 per square foot; large-format porcelain can be $8–$20/sq ft; natural stone like marble starts at $15/sq ft and up. Then add installation labor on top of material cost.

Shower Configuration

A prefab fiberglass shower insert costs $500–$2,000 installed. A custom tile shower with a frameless glass enclosure ranges from $5,000 to $15,000+. The difference is mostly labor and glass.

Plumbing Relocations

Moving the toilet, shower, or sink means rerouting supply and drain lines—add $2,000–$6,000 or more. Keeping fixtures in their existing locations is the most cost-effective approach.

Waterproofing

Proper waterproofing behind shower walls and on the floor is non-negotiable. It's not visible when the job is done, but skipping it leads to water damage, mold, and a costly redo. A licensed contractor will never skip this step.

💡 Pro Tip: Finalize your tile selection before demo begins. Tile often has lead times of 1–3 weeks, and last-minute changes to in-progress work are expensive. Bring your selections to the estimate meeting.

Permits & Hidden Costs

When a Permit Is Required:

  • Relocating plumbing lines (toilet, shower, sink)
  • Adding or moving electrical circuits or outlets
  • Installing a new exhaust fan requiring exterior venting
  • Any structural changes (moving walls, adding a window)

Cosmetic work—new tile over existing substrate, vanity swaps, fixture replacements—typically does not require a permit. But if your contractor opens walls, expect permits to be part of the scope.

⚠️ Heads Up: Bathroom water damage is one of the most common and costly home repair issues. Improperly waterproofed showers and unlicensed plumbing work are leading causes. Always verify a contractor's license at CSLB.ca.gov before hiring.

Budget for These Often-Overlooked Costs:

  • Demolition & disposal: $500–$2,000 to demo tile, remove fixtures, and haul debris
  • Subfloor repair: Water damage discovered during demo is common—budget $500–$2,500 as a contingency
  • Waterproofing membrane: $300–$800 for quality materials (Schluter, RedGard); never skip this
  • Contingency buffer: Add 10–15% to your total budget for surprises found behind walls or under tile

Typical Bathroom Remodel Timeline

Most bathroom remodels are faster than kitchens, but you'll still be without a functioning bathroom for a period:

Phase 1: Planning & Selection (1–3 weeks)

  • Contractor consultations and written estimates
  • Finalizing tile, fixtures, and vanity selections
  • Ordering materials with lead time in mind

Phase 2: Permits (3–10 business days)

  • Permit application (if required)
  • Approval varies by city and scope

Phase 3: Construction (1–3 weeks)

  • Demolition (1–2 days)
  • Rough plumbing and electrical (2–3 days)
  • Waterproofing and cement board (1–2 days + cure time)
  • Tile installation (3–7 days depending on scope)
  • Fixture installation, glass enclosure, vanity, and punch list (2–4 days)

Total from first call to finished bathroom: 4–8 weeks for most projects. Master bath renovations with custom work can run 8–12 weeks.

Plan ahead if this is your only full bathroom—you'll need an alternative during construction.

Return on Investment

Bathroom remodels are consistently one of the top-performing home improvement projects for resale value:

Average ROI by Remodel Tier:

  • Minor bathroom remodel (budget): 70–85% return at resale
  • Mid-range bathroom remodel: 60–70% return at resale
  • Upscale master bath remodel: 50–60% return at resale

Beyond resale numbers, an updated bathroom is one of the most-used rooms in the home. The daily quality-of-life improvement—hot water that stays hot, a shower that doesn't leak, tile that doesn't grout out—is often the more compelling argument for homeowners planning to stay long-term.

In San Diego County's real estate market, updated bathrooms are a strong selling point. Buyers factor renovation costs heavily when making offers, and a dated bathroom can drag down perceived home value more than its size suggests.

Why a Licensed Contractor Matters for Bathroom Work

Bathrooms involve water, electricity, and enclosed spaces—three things where shortcuts have serious consequences. Here's why licensing matters:

  • Waterproofing expertise: Improperly waterproofed showers cause structural damage, mold, and failed tile—often not visible until years later
  • Plumbing code compliance: Vent stacks, drain slopes, and fixture placement must meet code or inspections will fail
  • Electrical safety: GFCI outlets, proper circuits, and exhaust fans are code-required and safety-critical in wet environments
  • Insurance coverage: If water damage occurs from improper work, an unlicensed contractor leaves you with no recourse
  • Resale protection: Permitted, licensed work appears on records and reassures buyers—unpermitted work can kill a sale
⚠️ Warning: Bathroom water damage from faulty tile or plumbing work is one of the most expensive home repairs—easily $10,000–$40,000 to remediate properly. Hiring a licensed, insured contractor is not optional for shower and plumbing work.

What to Look for in a Bathroom Remodel Contractor:

  • Valid California contractor's license — verify at CSLB.ca.gov
  • Bonded and insured with general liability coverage
  • Experience with tile work, waterproofing systems, and plumbing
  • Portfolio of completed bathroom projects
  • Detailed written estimate with line-item breakdown
  • Clear timeline and communication throughout
